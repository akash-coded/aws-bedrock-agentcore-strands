# Cohort session template

*Copy this for a session of your own. The eight sessions in the [Cohort Kit](Cohort-Kit) all follow it.*

A session is ninety minutes and has one question. If it has two, it is two sessions. Everything below
serves the question: the pre-reading is what a person needs to argue about it, the exercise is where
they find out whether they can, and the decision is the answer written down.

---

## The template

```markdown
# Session N · <Title>

*<The one question this session answers, as a question.>*

**Pre-reading** (send one week ahead; about <NN> minutes)
- <Lesson title>(<site URL>) — <why it matters for this session, one clause>
- <Lesson title>(<site URL>) — <...>

**Bring.** <What each attendee brings: a candidate use case, last quarter's bill, an existing spec.>

**You will leave with.** <The artefact, named: an AI-fit verdict, a bar with its sample size, a bolt plan.>

## Run-sheet

| Minutes | Block | Do this | Material |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 | Recap | One question per pre-read lesson, answered by an attendee | — |
| 10–35 | The idea | Walk <picture> and <playbook page>; the room says what the picture shows | <links> |
| 35–65 | Exercise | Pairs work <exercise id> on the team's project; SkyWays if there is none. Compare answers before checking | <link to the exercise, the calculator> |
| 65–80 | Decision | The room decides <the decision> and writes it in <the template> | <link to the template> |
| 80–90 | Close | Homework named; one sentence each on what changed | — |

## Facilitator notes

- <The mistake this session's exercise usually surfaces, and what to say when it does.>
- <The place the room will want to skip ahead, and why not to.>
- <If the pre-reading was not done: the ten-minute version.>

## Homework

<One artefact, due before the next session, that the next session uses.>

## Next

**[Session N+1 · <Title>](<page>)** — <the question it answers>.
```

## How the pieces are chosen

**Pre-reading** is lessons on the [site](https://akash-coded.github.io/aws-bedrock-agentcore-strands/learn/),
never more than about forty minutes, because more than that is not read. Each lesson opens with its
answer, so a person who reads only the first paragraph of each still arrives able to take part.

**The idea block** uses one picture from the lesson and one page of the
[playbook](https://akash-coded.github.io/aws-bedrock-agentcore-strands/simulator/). The picture
carries the idea; the playbook page shows it with numbers that move. Put the picture on screen and ask
the room what it shows before you explain it. It takes longer and it lands.

**The exercise** comes from [Exercises and Answers](Exercises-and-Answers), which has thirty-one with
worked answers and a finder by role and difficulty, or from a playbook calculator run on the team's
own project. The rule for choosing: the exercise must be one a pair can get *wrong in an instructive
way*. An exercise everyone gets right is a recap, not an exercise.

**The decision** is the artefact of the step the session teaches, in the [template](https://akash-coded.github.io/aws-bedrock-agentcore-strands/templates/)
that role uses. Writing it down is the point; a decision that stays verbal is re-argued next week.

**Homework** is one thing, named, that the next session opens with. Never reading; reading is
pre-reading. Homework is an artefact.

## When a session goes wrong

| What happens | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Nobody did the pre-reading | Run the ten-minute version from the facilitator notes, then the exercise anyway. The exercise teaches the reading |
| The arithmetic takes the whole hour | Stop at thirty-five minutes with whatever numbers exist. The decision block works with rough numbers; the point is the assumption that differs, not the decimal |
| The room decides the candidate project is not an agent | That is a successful session. Write the verdict down in the AI-fit template and pick the next candidate for homework |
| One person knows all the answers | Make them the scribe for the decision block. They stop answering and start recording what the room decides |
| The team wants to skip to building | Session 3 is the earliest that makes sense, and only after the bar from session 2 exists. Say so, and say why: a bolt without a bar cannot be proven |
