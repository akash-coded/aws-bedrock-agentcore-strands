# Cohort Kit

*Eight ninety-minute sessions that turn the tutorial, the playbook and the course into a programme for a team. Pre-reading before, decisions during, one artefact after.*

The [tutorial](https://akash-coded.github.io/aws-bedrock-agentcore-strands/learn/) teaches the method
to one reader. A cohort learns it differently: the arithmetic lands when two people get different
answers, the gates land when somebody has to chair one, and the roles land when the room contains
more than one. This kit is the facilitator's side of that. Every session follows the same shape, so
the second one is easier to run than the first.

**Who it is for.** Anyone running this for a team: an engineering manager, a delivery lead, a trainer,
a study group that wants structure. You do not need to have run an agentic project; you need to have
read the pre-reading one week ahead of the room.

**What a cohort needs.** A projector for the [playbook](https://akash-coded.github.io/aws-bedrock-agentcore-strands/simulator/),
one laptop per two people, the pre-reading done, and a real candidate project from the team's own
backlog. The sessions keep returning to it. Without one, the exercises use SkyWays, which works but
lands less hard.

| 3 | **[Build, run and the loops](Cohort-Session-3-Build-Run-and-Loops)** | How is it built in proven slices, run, and kept honest? | Fundamentals 6–9 |
| 4 | **[Methods decoded](Cohort-Session-4-Methods-Decoded)** | Where do AI-DLC, AIDD, BMAD and spec-driven development actually sit? | Methods decoded |
| 5 | **[Plan and review](Cohort-Session-5-Plan-and-Review)** | How is the work planned, boarded and reviewed when a model writes most of the code? | Running delivery 1–5 |
| 6 | **[Prove and ship](Cohort-Session-6-Prove-and-Ship)** | How accurate must it be, how is that proven, and how does it reach production? | Running delivery 6–9 |

One session a week is the intended pace. Two a week works for a team that is about to start a project;
one a fortnight loses the thread. The [twelve-week reading group](Study-Plans) on the Study Plans page
is the course-shaped alternative, built on the modules rather than the tutorial; the two can run in
parallel with the same people.

## The shape every session shares

| Minutes | Block | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–10 | **Recap and pre-reading check** | One question per lesson, answered by a person not the facilitator. Anyone who has not read is paired, not excluded |
| 10–35 | **The idea** | The facilitator walks one picture from the tutorial and one page of the playbook. Not a lecture: the picture is on screen and the room says what it shows |
| 35–65 | **The exercise** | Pairs work a numbered exercise from [Exercises and Answers](Exercises-and-Answers) or a playbook calculator on the team's own project. Answers are compared before they are checked |
| 65–80 | **The decision** | The room takes one decision the session was building to and writes it down in the template that step uses |
| 80–90 | **Homework and close** | The homework is one artefact, named. The close is one sentence each: what changed in how you would do it |

The [session template](Cohort-Session-Template) has this as a copyable run-sheet, with the facilitator
notes that apply to every session.

## Facilitating it

- **Compare before you check.** When two pairs have different numbers, put both on the board and ask
  the room which assumption differs. The answer key is for afterwards.
- **Keep the candidate project in the room.** Every exercise that can be done on the team's own
  backlog is done on it. The SkyWays figures are the fallback, not the default.
- **Let the unwelcome answer stand.** Several exercises resolve to "this is not an agent" or "this
  bar cannot be met". A cohort that learns to say so has learned the most expensive lesson first.
- **Time-box the arithmetic, not the argument.** The calculators take two minutes; the disagreement
  about the damage figure is the session.
- **End on an artefact.** A session that ends with a written decision, however small, is retained.
  One that ends with a discussion is not.

## After the eighth session

The team has: an AI-fit verdict on a real candidate, a bar with its sample size, a spec with the
decisions left to the builder marked, a bolt plan, a review policy by risk band, a two-number report
and a gate they have chaired. That is the evidence pack for P0 and P1 of their own project, which is
the point.

Write a [field note](Field-Notes) about how it went. The sessions here are designed and not yet
field-tested; the first note from a facilitator changes them more than another draft would.
