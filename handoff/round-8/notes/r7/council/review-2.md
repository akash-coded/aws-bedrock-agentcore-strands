# Review 2: the teacher's read

My own frames are in `SP/r7/rev2/`.

## Part 1. The answers

**Strongest: A.** It turned "fluid, elegant" into a picture that could be built, and the prototype proves it: no fragments in any frame, three shapes that read at about 45px (`p3` t0, t2, t5), and a sound still frame (`rev2/still-1440.jpg`). The outline that turns solid at the sign-off is the one thing in the hero that teaches. Its rule, "never hide words to animate them in", protects reading. C comes second: it found that the sketches carry no paint of their own.

**Biggest blind spot: E.** It judges by looks, not by what a reader learns. A neutral hairline removes the colours the phase line depends on, and "no shape change" refuses the owner's own request on a guess the prototype disproves.

**All five missed two things.**

1. The way back. In `p3` t6 no aircraft is in sight and no phase is lit, for about nine seconds in every 29. The loop back to Frame is the point of the spiral, and nothing names it.
2. Their sketch lists contradict each other. `push-one-side-down`, `tie-off-the-rope` and `pulled-up-to-see-the-roots` are among E's twelve best and on D's or B's cut list. "Cover the caption" is one reader's guess, so no single list should be carried out.

## Part 2. Verdicts

1. **Sketches.** No quota. Cut the ten that three or more advisors named (`the-last-sure-thread`, `lift-the-blanket`, `a-glass-on-the-wild-mushrooms`, `what-comes-out-of-the-exhaust`, `a-stand-in-the-right-size`, `something-soft-underneath`, `only-the-chips-it-holds`, `raw-fish-gets-its-own-board`, `the-prompter-in-the-box`, `order-written-from-the-jacket`) and the eight that two named and nobody praised. Then one drawing per idea, two per lesson at most, never two in one section. About 72 survive. A quota of one cuts good pictures from lessons that teach two ideas.

2. **Home sample.** Keep it, and swap it for `the-needle-never-shakes`. It is the only proof of what a lesson feels like, it reads with no context, and it comes from lesson one, which the button beside it opens.

3. **Shape change.** Keep it as the prototype has it. The owner asked for it in so many words, and the frames show a clean dart, airliner and jet in both themes.

4. **Coils and colour.** Three coils, with the four hues on the live coil only. The hues are the key the phase line is read against. Draw the two older coils plain grey with no tick: two or three rose ticks (`p3` t0, t7) look like a doubled image, and there is one sign-off.

5. **Earth.** Keep 75 seconds. The owner called slow a fault, and in the frames the land drifts the same way as the aircraft at about half its speed, so it reads as ground passing under a flight.

6. **Boards on `/method/`.** Remove the reveal. The cells are numbered, so the order is already on the page (`rev2/method-dark-1440-02.jpg`), and a watchdog admits that content can wait.

7. **Page changes.** Remove them entirely. A cross-fade teaches nothing, Firefox shows none, and a reader wants the next lesson at once.

8. **Section two.** Move the funnel, whole, to the top of the methods section, above the table. A heading must be answered by the first thing under it: on a phone "four places" is followed by four method names (`rev2/home-dark-390-02.jpg`), and the methods are the subject of the next heading.

9. **Phone counts.** Drop the line on phones. The same numbers come back in the tutorial heading and on the library cards, and under the picture it would be a second caption beside the phase line.

10. **Prototype changes.**
    1. Phone. At 390 the globe still starts at 630 of 844 and is cut by the first screen, the phase line falls on the second screen, and the pause button covers the jet in P3 (`rev2/home-dark-390-t3.jpg`). Make the globe about 220px and put pause beside the phase line.
    2. Name the way back. Add "back to Frame" as the last quiet item in the phase line, lit while the aircraft is behind the globe. In `p3` t7 the dart is already in sight and Frame is still unlit.
    3. The whole scene, phase line included, is `aria-hidden`. Give it one hidden sentence that says what the picture shows.
